What do you need for an EB-1A petition?

EB-1A eligibility requires evidence of extraordinary ability. A qualifying major, internationally recognised award or evidence meeting at least three of ten criteria addresses the initial evidentiary stage; the record as a whole must also establish the required sustained recognition and standing in the field.

  • A step-by-step preparation process
  • Reviewing achievements

First, identify achievements relevant to the EB-1A criteria. These may include nationally or internationally recognised awards.

Membership in qualifying professional organisations.

Media coverage about you and your work.

Scholarly articles in qualifying publications.

Leading or critical work on significant projects, where the applicable requirements are met.

Tip: start with a complete list of your professional achievements to identify the strongest parts of your record.

Selecting criteria

Select the criteria that fit your documented record. A scientist may rely on scholarly articles, awards or peer review; an IT professional may have evidence of significant contributions, critical roles or coverage; a beauty professional may have awards, judging or professional coverage. Each example needs to meet the specific criterion rather than merely match a broad topic.

Gathering evidence

Provide supporting documents for every criterion relied on. Award evidence may include certificates, photographs and official records; membership evidence may include admission rules and confirmation; publication evidence may include the complete article and outlet information. Recommendation letters should explain specific achievements. Foreign-language documents need complete English translations with the required certification.

Preparing recommendation letters

Recommendation letters can explain evidence and context. They should come from people with a sound basis for their statements and describe specific contributions, their professional impact and how the writer knows your work. General praise cannot replace documentary support.

Working with professional publications

If your publication record needs strengthening, start with your actual expertise. Possible topics include an analysis of methods in your field, a documented project case study or your contribution to an industry development. Whether an article supports a criterion depends on its content and the publication; being outside academia does not remove those requirements.

Organising the case

Organise the documents so the evidence is easy to follow. Group materials by criterion and add explanations and cross-references. An index, contents page and consistent exhibit labels help the reader locate supporting documents.

Common preparation mistakes

Common problems include evidence that does not substantiate the claimed contribution, inaccurate or incomplete translations, and relying on criteria the documents do not meet. Careful review can identify these gaps before filing.

Practical advice

Start early. Gathering documents and preparing publication materials can take several months.

Ask appropriate specialists to review your profile and discuss the strategy with your attorney.

How achievements are presented matters, but the underlying evidence and legal eligibility remain essential.
