Can ChatGPT help build an EB-1A case? Common mistakes explained

AI can help organise materials, but EB-1A and O-1 cases require careful work with the criteria. We explain where AI falls short and the risks this creates.

Why people consider building a case with AI

Preparing an EB-1A or O-1 petition can look like a writing task: describe achievements, draft letters and address the criteria. Many people therefore try to do it themselves with ChatGPT or another AI tool. This can work for a first draft. Problems arise when the case becomes a document with legal consequences.

Where AI helps

  • organising materials and listing questions about your achievements;
  • producing a draft translation of documents;
  • making the writing consistent and checking spelling;
  • drafting letters that a person then reviews.

Where AI falls short

1. AI does not know exactly how USCIS applies the criteria

A criterion is a rule with specific conditions. For example, published material about you must focus on you, rather than merely mention your name. AI can produce persuasive wording that does not satisfy the criterion. Read more in our explanation of the published-material criterion.

2. Invented or inaccurate sources

Language models sometimes produce convincing references, quotations and facts that are not real. Every claim in a petition needs documentary support. A single nonexistent source can undermine confidence in the whole case.

3. Generic wording

AI-generated drafts often rely on general phrases such as exceptional professional, significant contribution or industry recognition. The officer needs facts: figures, scale, independent evidence and context.

4. No connection between the criteria

A strong case connects its evidence: the criteria support one another and the overall argument that you belong among the leaders in your field. AI may draft sections separately and miss contradictions.

5. No understanding of the final merits determination

First, the officer assesses the initial evidentiary requirements, then considers the evidence as a whole. Read more in our article on what the officer looks for.

What can happen to a self-prepared case

Common consequences of weak preparation include:

  1. a request for evidence (RFE) and additional time;
  2. having to rework some materials;
  3. in the worst case, denial and the need to start again.

This does not mean you cannot prepare a case yourself. It means mistakes can be costly, and some are easier to spot with regular experience of these cases.

What to do if you have already used AI for a draft

It is better to have a specialist review the draft before filing. During a consultation, we examine how the text addresses the criteria, where the wording is weak, which documents are missing and how to prioritise improvements. Addressing gaps before an RFE can save time and cost.

Frequently asked questions

Can I file a petition written by AI?

There is no formal blanket ban, but you are responsible for every claim. Inaccurate wording, invented references or a mismatch with the criteria can lead to a request for evidence (RFE) or denial.

What can AI safely help with in a case?

A draft structure, questions about your achievements, translation, proofreading and consistent document formatting. A qualified specialist should review the final reasoning and legally significant wording.

How can I tell whether my draft is weak?

If it describes you in general terms, does not address a specific criterion and lacks supporting documents, an officer can identify those gaps. An independent review before filing is the most reliable check.
